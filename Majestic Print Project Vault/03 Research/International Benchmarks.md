# International benchmarks

Six relevant reference companies; this is a pattern shortlist, not an objective ranking of the world's best companies. Pages inspected through official-site text, not full transactional testing. Sources accessed on 5 October 2026.

## MOO — make material quality understandable

**Observed:** Business-card pages organise premium options and explain distinct papers/finishes. Sample packs support physical comparison; design freedom and selected fast-delivery options are selling points.

**Adapt:** close-up finish photography, a concise paper comparison, and a sample enquiry if Majestic can fulfil it. **Avoid:** copying MOO's brand style or promising its sample/delivery policies.

Sources: [Business cards](https://www.moo.com/us/business-cards), [Sample packs](https://www.moo.com/us/sample-packs), [Paper](https://www.moo.com/us/about/paper).

## VistaPrint — guided routes for non-designers

**Observed:** Product pages offer template browsing and artwork upload, with paper, corner and quantity choices. Design-service support provides another route.

**Adapt:** distinguish “I have artwork” from “I need design help.” Provide plain option descriptions. **Defer:** a large browser template editor until its commercial value, licensing and staff workflow are confirmed.

Sources: [Standard business cards](https://www.vistaprint.com/business-cards/standard), [Templates](https://www.vistaprint.com/business-cards/standard/templates).

## UPrinting — reduce file and production uncertainty

**Observed:** Offers artwork upload, a file review, and PDF proof options. Its review guidance separates proof approval/payment from the start of production and distinguishes a screen proof from colour-accurate physical output.

**Adapt:** explain what a file check covers and require the correct proof version before printing. **Avoid:** implying software or a PDF eliminates all print errors or guarantees exact colour.

Sources: [Business cards](https://www.uprinting.com/business-cards.html), [Project review](https://www.uprinting.com/free_project_review.html).

## Solopress — product options and delivery conditions

**Observed:** Business-card selection includes different stocks/finishes, artwork templates, and delivery guidance with geography-specific exceptions. Account/quote navigation is also exposed.

**Adapt:** product-specific artwork downloads and explicit delivery conditions. **Avoid:** presenting print turnaround as the arrival date or copying UK delivery promises.

Source: [Business cards](https://www.solopress.com/business-cards/).

## Sticker Mule — focus each product's configuration

**Observed:** Die-cut stickers expose size, quantity, artwork upload, online proofing, material benefits, and product reviews together.

**Adapt:** a compact configurator with the next step visible and reviews relevant to the product. **Avoid:** treating a visual preview as an approved production proof or copying competitor ratings and turnaround promises.

Source: [Die-cut stickers](https://www.stickermule.com/products/die-cut-stickers).

## Gelato — separate different audience routes

**Observed:** Creator commerce and print-producer operations have separate calls to action. The site promotes local production through a global network and store integrations.

**Adapt:** a later business reorder/workspace route if repeat-order demand supports it. **Avoid:** expanding the initial Majestic project into a global fulfilment or marketplace platform.

Source: [Homepage](https://www.gelato.com/).
