# Brand-aligned research refresh

The guide changes the research question from “How should a print-and-gift shop look?” to “How should an integrated print, packaging, signage and promotional company help customers buy or specify the right job?”

The original audit remains evidence. Useful earlier references are retained. This pass adds official-site research for the capability areas the original shortlist underrepresented. No transactions or claims about actual competitor delivery/quality were tested.

## Added Sri Lankan references

### PrintWorks.lk — bespoke production

Its About page describes UV, laser, acrylic, signage and gift work and an enquiry route. It also explicitly excludes some bulk paper/book/FMCG work, despite broader terms elsewhere in its interface. Use its explanation of bespoke inputs as a reference, not its entire catalogue as a proxy for Majestic.

**Planning implication:** each solution needs a clear capability boundary and quote inputs, not a generic “we do everything” form. [Official About](https://printworks.lk/about).

### Aura Solutions — corporate gifts and branding methods

Its promotional-material page presents gift sourcing and branding through different printing methods, with a practical item range.

**Planning implication:** corporate campaigns need a bulk route with quantity, product choice and branding requirements; individual gift checkout is a separate route. [Promotional materials](https://www.aura.lk/advertising/promotional-materials).

### Signage/vehicle research lead

Thilina Stickers' official search excerpt describes vehicle, signage, CNC/laser and UV capability, but the opened page exposed no readable text. Keep it as a limited lead rather than claiming its interface or ordering flow was verified. [Official site](https://www.thilinastickers.com/).

## Added international references

### Packlane — packaging specifications

Its page combines packaging style/size/material configuration, dieline guidance, proofs and a custom-run quote route. It distinguishes production and transit time. Its published proof policy includes automatic approval after a delay; **do not adopt that rule** for Majestic's proposed explicit-approval workflow.

**Planning implication:** packaging needs its own input model and proof ownership; a 3D editor is optional. [Official site](https://packlane.com/).

### Signs.com — organise by output and application

Its site surfaces sign/banner/decal products and design assistance. This supports separating customer-visible products from internal processes.

**Planning implication:** display actual sign types and use cases, then ask the appropriate installation questions. Competitor free-design/guarantee claims are not Majestic policies. [Official site](https://www.signs.com/).

## Resulting changes

1. Use the exact guide tagline and all-in-one positioning.
2. Add a Solutions hub covering all 12 families beside the product shop.
3. Treat packaging, signage, UV, CNC/laser and vehicle branding as explicit planned pages.
4. Give corporate gifts a bulk/campaign route as well as individual products.
5. Keep the homepage balanced across **Print / Pack / Promote**, with personal gifts visible.
6. Keep prices, turnaround, proof and delivery promises unconfirmed until approved.
